# Analysing Customer Feedback in Discord  
**Discord Feedback Summary – Focus Bear**

## Overview

I reviewed messages and discussions from the customer feedback channels in Focus Bear’s Discord over the past month to identify recurring themes, feature requests, and sentiment. Discord feedback tends to be informal, candid, and emotionally driven, which makes it especially useful for understanding friction points that users may not raise through formal support channels.

This analysis focuses on patterns rather than individual comments, highlighting what users repeatedly struggle with, request, or appreciate.

---

## Why Discord Is Valuable for User Feedback

Users often prefer Discord for feedback because:
- It feels conversational and low-friction.
- Users can validate their experiences by seeing others face similar issues.
- Feedback is often shared in real time, close to the moment of frustration or delight.

Compared to support tickets, Discord feedback is less polished but more honest, making it a strong signal for early product issues and unmet expectations.

---

## Recurring User Requests & Pain Points

### 1. Confusion Around Permissions & Blocking Behaviour
**Pattern observed**
- Multiple users expressed uncertainty about why certain permissions are required.
- Users were unsure what would happen once Focus or Super Strict Mode was enabled.

**Example themes**
- “Why is this permission needed?”
- “I didn’t realise this would block so much.”
- “Can I preview what will be blocked?”

**Impact**
This confusion creates anxiety during onboarding and can lead to users quitting early rather than experimenting.

---

### 2. Desire for More Control & Flexibility in Focus Modes
**Pattern observed**
- Users appreciate strict blocking but want clearer control.
- Requests for easier toggling, previews, or gentler modes were common.

**Example themes**
- Ability to temporarily relax restrictions.
- Clearer distinction between Focus Mode and Super Strict Mode.
- Warnings before entering highly restrictive sessions.

**Impact**
Users want commitment *with confidence*, not surprises.

---

### 3. UI & UX Polish Requests (Especially on Mobile)
**Pattern observed**
- Repeated mentions of small but distracting UI issues.
- Users notice spacing, truncation, and inconsistent behaviour quickly.

**Example themes**
- Cut-off headers.
- Timer scroll behaviour feeling abrupt.
- Inconsistent visual language across screens.

**Impact**
These issues don’t break functionality, but they reduce trust, especially for first-time users.

---

## Highlighted User Suggestion (Deep Dive)

### Suggestion:  
**“Show a clear summary of what will happen before starting a focus session.”**

#### What problem does this solve?
- Reduces uncertainty and fear of losing control.
- Helps users mentally prepare before committing.
- Addresses repeated confusion around permissions and blocking.

#### Does this fit Focus Bear’s roadmap?
Yes. This aligns strongly with:
- Focus Bear’s mission to support neurodivergent users.
- Ongoing efforts to improve onboarding clarity.
- Reducing early churn without changing core functionality.

#### Potential Challenges
- Designing a summary that is informative without overwhelming.
- Ensuring consistency across mobile, desktop, and web.
- Avoiding extra steps that slow experienced users.

A solution could be optional or shown only during the first few sessions.

---

## Reflection

### Three Common User Concerns or Requests
1. Clearer explanations around permissions and blocking.
2. Better understanding of focus modes before activation.
3. Ongoing UX polish, especially on mobile.

### Discord vs Support Tickets (Zoho Desk)
- **Discord** feedback is more emotional, immediate, and exploratory.
- **Support tickets** are more specific, problem-focused, and transactional.
Together, they provide a full picture: Discord shows *friction*, support shows *failure points*.

### One Suggestion That Stood Out
The request for a **pre-focus summary** stood out because it appeared across multiple conversations and directly connects user anxiety with early drop-off behaviour seen in analytics.

---

## Final Thoughts

Discord feedback reinforces what analytics and onboarding feedback already suggest: users believe in Focus Bear’s mission but need more clarity and reassurance early on. Treating Discord as a pattern-detection tool rather than a feature request list allows PMs to extract high-impact improvements without chasing noise.
